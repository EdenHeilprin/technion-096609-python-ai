# Collaboration and revision

Status: working policy draft

## Learning together

Discussion, peer explanation, debugging help, and comparison of approaches are encouraged. AI tools are also permitted under the course AI policy.

Unless a task is designated as pair/group work, the submitted artifact and explanation must remain your own accountable work. Do not give or receive another student's private submission as a substitute for doing the task.

## Appropriate collaboration on individual work

- discuss concepts, errors, and general strategies;
- point to course documentation or a relevant example;
- help someone construct a small synthetic test case;
- explain how to read a traceback;
- review a short excerpt while the author remains the decision-maker;
- compare outputs after both students have attempted the task.

## Inappropriate collaboration on individual work

- sharing a complete current solution or private repository for copying;
- editing another student's assessed file until it becomes your implementation;
- submitting another person's code, explanation, checks, or AI work note as your own;
- using an unreleased solution or hidden test without authorization;
- misrepresenting who completed an assessed demonstration.

The relevant distinction is accountable ownership, not whether help occurred.

## Pair capstones

Both partners are responsible for the whole project's behavior, data contract, reproducibility, and privacy—not only the files they edited. Use repository history and a short division-of-work note for coordination, not as a simplistic measure of intellectual contribution. Each student completes an individual demonstration or equivalent ownership check.

Raise a non-participating or unsafe collaboration pattern early. The instructor may adjust team scope, request individual evidence, or separate a team when needed.

## Revision

Weekly mastery tasks normally include a correction opportunity before the published cut-off. Revision is expected to respond to evidence:

1. identify what failed or remained unclear;
2. change the smallest relevant part;
3. rerun the program and checks;
4. state what now supports the result.

Replacing an entire file with a generated alternative may be permitted, but it still requires inspection, verification, and explanation. The official task page states whether and how a revised grade replaces the original.

Practical checks and capstone milestones use the specific correction/recovery rules published with those assessments.
