# AI use and accountability

Status: working policy draft; institutional rules may require revision

## The short version

AI tools are permitted throughout this course, including in graded work. You may use chats, autocomplete, and coding agents to explain, plan, generate, debug, review, test, document, and revise.

Permission to use assistance does not transfer responsibility. You are responsible for every file, claim, output, decision, and disclosure in your submission.

## What unrestricted use means

- There is no course rule limiting AI to a particular percentage of code.
- There is no requirement to begin each graded task without AI.
- Complete chat transcripts are not required.
- Using AI is not itself evidence of misconduct or weaker learning.
- Choosing not to use AI does not earn automatic extra credit.
- The course will not rely on AI-output detectors to assign grades.

Different tools may have different institutional, privacy, security, and licensing conditions. “Permitted by the course” does not authorize uploading material you are not allowed to share.

## Your responsibilities

Before submitting, you should be able to:

1. run the artifact from the documented starting point;
2. explain its important behavior and data flow;
3. identify assumptions and plausible failure cases;
4. show checks that distinguish plausible output from correct output;
5. make or reason through a bounded adjacent change;
6. state known limitations honestly;
7. confirm that sharing with the tools/services used was permitted.

“The AI produced it” is useful process information, but it is not a correctness argument.

## A recommended working loop

> **Specify → attempt → ask → inspect → predict → test → revise → explain → disclose**

This is an instructional workflow, not a restriction. As your skills develop, AI may handle larger changes. Your specification, review, tests, privacy decisions, and recovery plan must develop with it.

## Short AI work note

When AI use substantially affects a submission, include a concise note such as:

```text
Tools: [tool/model if known]
Purpose: [what I used it for]
Consequential contribution: [one important suggestion/change]
Verification: [how I checked it]
```

Routine autocomplete and trivial definitions do not need item-by-item logging. The note is not a transcript and is not intended to calculate how much code is “human-written.” If no AI was used substantively, say so in one sentence when the task requests a work note.

## Assessment with AI available

Assignments are designed so that generating plausible code is not the entire task. Evidence may include:

- a working, rerunnable artifact;
- exact output or public/hidden checks;
- test cases and edge cases;
- explanation of choices and limitations;
- diagnosis of a defect;
- a bounded live or recorded modification;
- comparison of alternatives.

You may use AI during these activities unless an institutionally necessary exception is announced explicitly. An explanation or modification request is a normal evidence-of-ownership activity, not an accusation.

## Privacy and security

Never give an AI service:

- passwords, tokens, API keys, or credentials;
- identifiable participant, student, employee, or client data;
- confidential research material or unpublished data without explicit authorization;
- private repository content when the service is not approved for it;
- copyrighted or licensed material you are not allowed to redistribute.

Use synthetic/minimal examples when asking for help. Follow the [privacy and research-data policy](privacy-and-data.md).

## Equity and paid tools

Required course outcomes will not depend on purchasing an individual AI subscription. The course will publish one free or institutionally funded path for required activities. Paid tools and higher usage limits may be used when available, but they should not create a grading advantage unrelated to the learning outcomes.

If the required baseline service is unavailable, the instructor will provide a fallback, additional time, or an equivalent route. Report access barriers early through the private course contact route.

## Collaboration

AI permission does not erase the distinction between tool assistance and another student's assessed work. Do not copy private student repositories, submissions, or solution files. Follow the task's human-collaboration rules and the [collaboration and revision policy](collaboration-and-revision.md).

## If something goes wrong

If a tool exposes confidential material, changes files outside the intended scope, deletes work, or introduces a security issue:

1. stop the operation;
2. preserve enough non-sensitive information to understand what occurred;
3. disconnect or revoke affected credentials when relevant;
4. notify the instructor through the private route;
5. use version history/backups to recover safely;
6. do not repost the sensitive material as part of the help request.

Responsible early reporting is part of professional practice.
