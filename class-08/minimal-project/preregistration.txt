SURE OR GAMBLE — TEACHING PREREGISTRATION

This is a classroom exercise, not an externally registered study.
The plan is prepared before collecting the classroom responses.

QUESTION AND PREDICTION
Do people choose a gamble less often when the guaranteed alternative is larger?
We predict a generally decreasing percentage of Gamble choices as the sure offer increases.

PROCEDURE
Each participant makes six choices, in this order: 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, and 8 sure points.
The gamble always offers a 50% chance of 10 points and a 50% chance of 0 points.
Choices are hypothetical. There are no payments or identifying questions.

DATA
Use only the classroom session, keeping rehearsal sessions separate.
Each CSV row contains a session code, participant code, sure offer, and choice.
A blank choice means no response was recorded for that offer.

ANALYSIS
Include every recorded Sure or Gamble choice; do not replace missing choices.
Report the number of participants with at least one recorded choice.
For each offer, count Gamble choices and divide by the number of recorded choices at that offer.
Plot these percentages against the six offers. Show the counts as well as the percentages.
Describe whether the pattern follows the prediction; do not run a significance test.

INTERPRETATION
Because everyone sees the offers in the same increasing order, offer size and position change together.
The graph describes this classroom's choices; it cannot separate those two influences.
