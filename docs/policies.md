## AI use

AI chats, autocomplete, and coding agents are permitted throughout the course, including graded work, unless Moodle explicitly identifies an exceptional institutional restriction.

There is no percentage limit on AI-generated code and no routine requirement to submit complete chat transcripts. Using AI is not automatically penalized; avoiding AI is not automatically rewarded.

You remain responsible for:

- running and checking the artifact;
- explaining consequential behavior, assumptions, and limitations;
- using tests or known cases that distinguish plausible from correct output;
- making or reasoning through a bounded adjacent change;
- protecting data, credentials, and private materials;
- providing a concise AI work note when requested.

Recommended loop:

> **Specify → attempt → ask → inspect → predict → test → revise → explain → disclose**

## Tool access

Required participation will not depend on purchasing an individual AI subscription. When a specific tool is required, the course will provide a free, institutionally funded, shared-observation, or equivalent fallback route.

## Privacy

Use synthetic or explicitly approved public data in course exercises, demonstrations, public repositories, and ordinary AI requests.

Never upload:

- passwords, access tokens, API keys, or private links;
- identifiable student, participant, employee, or client data;
- grades or identity mappings;
- confidential or unpublished research material without authorization;
- private repository content to a service not approved for it;
- hidden checks or unreleased solutions.

Replacing a name does not automatically anonymize a real record. When uncertain, construct a genuinely invented example and contact the instructor privately.

## Collaboration

Discussion, peer explanation, general strategy, and privacy-safe debugging help are encouraged.

On individual work, do not share a complete current solution for copying, edit another student's assessed artifact until it becomes their implementation, use an unreleased solution without authorization, or misrepresent who completed an individual demonstration.

The central distinction is accountable ownership—not whether assistance occurred.

## Asking for technical help

Include:

- operating system;
- exact file you ran;
- how you ran it;
- expected behavior;
- complete error text;
- smallest relevant synthetic code;
- what you already tried.

Never post credentials, identifiers, confidential data, or private links. Use the Moodle private-contact route when a safe public example is impossible.

Institutional policy and the official Moodle course take precedence.
